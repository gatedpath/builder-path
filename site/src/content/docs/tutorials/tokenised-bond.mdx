<StepCard n={2} title="Decide what a bond adds, and what it must not touch">
<StepPart kind="gets">
A design you can defend before you write a line: what is inherited, what is new, and the two rules the money has to obey.
</StepPart>
<StepPart kind="do">
Open `contracts/src/GatedERC20.sol`. Everything a bond needs for compliance is already there: both parties of every movement are checked against the verifier, an issuer mints inside a time-boxed allowance, the compliance role can move units with a justification hash on the log, and a pauser can stop everything. A bond inherits all of it and changes none of it. Bind it to request id 708, the AU wholesale investor recipe in `recipes/au-wholesale-investor/`, whose privacy note is worth reading first: on a public chain that proof says something financial about a person.

What the bond adds is coupons, and the first decision is how not to pay them. Paying every holder in a loop has no upper bound on gas, and [Review before mainnet](/review/) treats an unbounded loop as fatal. So the paying agent funds one number, and holders claim. For that to be fair, the contract has to see every balance change, so that a unit earns only the coupons funded while its holder held it. There is one place every movement passes through, `_update`, and the kit marks it `virtual` for exactly this.

Then the two rules. The coupon is fixed per unit, so the contract accumulates that fixed amount and never divides a coupon by the supply; a holder of whole units is owed an exact figure, to the last base unit of the stablecoin. And funding rounds up while claims round down, so every coupon brings in at least what it creates in claims. I did not start with either rule. The run below found both.
</StepPart>
<StepPart kind="worked">
You can say, without looking, why a holder who buys after a coupon does not receive it, and why the contract can never owe more than it holds.
</StepPart>
</StepCard>

<TabItem label="Writing Solidity">
Four new files, and no change to any file the scaffold gave you.

`contracts/src/TokenisedBond.sol` inherits `GatedERC20` and passes a subscription amount of zero, because a bond is issued by its issuer through `mint` and is not claimed by whoever turns up. The schedule is fixed in the constructor: the stablecoin, the coupon per whole unit, when the first coupon falls due, the period, and how many coupons there are. A `PAYING_AGENT_ROLE` funds them, kept apart from the admin so that the account holding the stablecoin is not the account that can change roles.

The hook is the heart of it. `super._update` first, always, because it is the eligibility check and the pause:

Funding a coupon computes the amount from the supply at that moment and never takes it as an argument. It is refused before its due time, after the last coupon, while paused, and when no units exist to receive it:

`claimCoupons` carries the `gated` modifier, so a holder must be eligible at the moment it claims. A holder whose credential has lapsed loses nothing: the amount waits.

Then `contracts/test/TokenisedBond.t.sol`, with a six-decimal `contracts/test/mocks/MockStable.sol`. The test that earns its place is the last one. It does not check an example; it holds the rule that the contract can always pay what it owes, and then makes every holder actually claim:

Last, `contracts/script/DeployBond.s.sol`, which runs the scaffold's pre-flight and then deploys. Off mainnet it deploys a mock verifier when `VERIFIER` is unset; on a local chain only, it deploys the mock stablecoin when `STABLECOIN` is unset, and says so in capitals. ## What you learned

The gated ERC-20 shape is a base to build on, not a finished product: the bond added 196 lines and changed none of the token's. Scheduled payments belong in accounting, not in loops. And the two faults worth remembering were both in money arithmetic that looked standard: a division that was not needed, and a rounding that went the wrong way. Neither was found by reading. Both were found by a test that expected an exact number.

What this page does not give you is a bond you could issue. No static analyser has read the new contract, the principal is never redeemed, and the stablecoin in the rehearsal mints to anyone. Two design limits belong beside those. A coupon goes to whoever holds the units when it is funded, not when it falls due, so a paying agent who is late moves a coupon from the seller to the buyer; a real bond fixes a record date. And the accounting assumes the stablecoin delivers the amount it was asked to move; a token that takes a fee on transfer would leave the bond short. The <Ext href="https://github.com/gatedpath/builder-path/blob/main/cards/runs/2026-09-18/card-05.md">run record</Ext> lists what is not done, and why.
