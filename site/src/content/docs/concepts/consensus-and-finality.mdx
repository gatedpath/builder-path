DBFT has no leader: every governor proposes, the proposals are combined into a super block, and transactions are "finalised in seconds". Vine's page has the diagram; the [DBFT and Red Belly papers](/research/) have the proofs.

## What this means for your code

A ported dApp usually carries "wait for N confirmations" logic, a reorg handler in the indexer, and a UI state for "pending, might be dropped", and none of that has a job here, so you can delete it. A transaction that is in a block is final, so keep the pending state only for the seconds between send and receipt.

With no reorgs, idempotency becomes your job, because the only way an event gets processed twice is your own retry, a restarted worker, or a duplicated webhook. Key your off-chain writes on the transaction hash and log index, and make every handler safe to run twice. On this network that covers everything "reorg safety" used to mean, and it is less work than what you had.

The other side of finality is that there is no confirmation window in which to notice a mistake, and a bad transaction that lands has landed. I would test on [a fork](/start/) first, then on testnet with an [unverified and an ineligible wallet](/concepts/identity-gate/), before the real one.

Do not build on order within a block. The Red Belly paper does document a rule (Section V.C.1): each governor batches its oldest transactions first, the super block takes the accepted batches starting from a governor that rotates with every block, each batch keeps its own order, and a transaction that conflicts with one already taken is discarded. Two things stop me from building on it. The paper's chain is a UTXO payment system with no EVM, so it does not say how two contract calls that touch the same storage are ordered. And Redbelly told me on 17 September 2026 that the ordering changes in Q4 2026 and will be described in the next yellow paper (open [questions 5 and 38](/research/#the-questions-this-site-cites)). The current yellow paper, version 1.0 of November 2024, says the transactions in a decided set are executed in the order of their hash or nonce, which is their stated rule until the next version. So a contract must not assume that transaction A in a block ran before transaction B, and a front end must not assume that its two sends land in send order.

The measured priority fee is zero on both networks, and the base fee is set by a price oracle rather than by demand, so there is no fee-market griefing and no priority-fee front-running. What remains sits with the governors, who are identified and staked: a governor chooses the order inside its own batch. The paper proves that a correctly submitted transaction is eventually included; it does not prove that the order is fair. Gas-limit denial of service inside a single transaction is unchanged, and an unbounded loop is still fatal.

## Where the guarantee stops

"Formally verified" has an exact scope here, and I keep to it. What was checked by machine (Holistic Verification, DISC 2022) is the binary consensus at the heart of DBFT, as pseudocode, for any number of nodes with fewer than a third faulty: safety without conditions, liveness under a fairness assumption. The node software, the EVM and your contract are outside that proof, and so is the step that turns many proposals into one super block.

"Cannot fork" has a scope too: fewer than a third of governors faulty. Above that line the design detects the guilty from their own signed messages (Polygraph, ICDCS 2021), and the design Redbelly cites for "tolerates a colluding majority" (ZLB, DSN 2024) then forks, votes the guilty out, votes new members in, merges the branches and recomputes state, with a contract's returned state reverted and reimbursed if it was on the losing branch. That is a recovery, and a stronger one than a chain without accountability has, but it is not the same promise as the first regime. Redbelly's own yellow paper carries both sentences, "pending or committed" and "can recover", without saying which the deployed network runs; I have that down as question 56 and will ask. Until it is answered, everything on this page assumes the first regime, which is the one every other chain assumes too, and the pause behind a Safe on the [review page](/review/) is the answer for the second.

Who the governors are, and how one is admitted, replaced or removed, is not described anywhere I can cite yet. Redbelly told me on 12 October 2026 that a new yellow paper, due this quarter, describes the process, and that the rule will be a sufficient stake, open to anyone, under the Digital Asset Platform law; the founder said in June 2026 that an accountable consensus ships in the next release that is not backward compatible. Until the paper is out, this page cites no research paper for who validates today, because the research designs and the deployed network do not obviously share a membership model ([question 44](/research/#the-questions-this-site-cites)).
