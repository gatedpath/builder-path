## What this means for your code

One read, in two places. Pre-flight reads `isAllowed` for the deployer on the target chain before any deploy. Your front end reads it for the connected wallet before offering a send, and when it is false it shows one thing, a link to the Access dApp. Do not retry, and do not fall back to another wallet or hide the state.

A key is an identity. On Ethereum a stolen key loses funds; here it also impersonates a verified person or business. Every admin role sits behind a Safe with a threshold of at least two, and Safe 1.4.1 is at its canonical addresses on both networks. High-value user actions should prompt a hardware wallet. Rotate where you can, and rehearse the pause.

Two chains, one key. Chain 151 and 153 share your deployer key and your credentials. Any signature your contracts accept, EIP-712 or permits, must bind the chain ID, and there should be a test that a testnet signature fails on mainnet.

Decide revocation now. If a holder's credential is revoked while they hold a position, does the asset freeze, move to custody, or stay put? What happens to a transfer approved against a credential at block N and revoked at N+1? The network's behaviour on revocation is not documented yet (questions 23 and 24 are with Redbelly), so your contract has to be explicit either way and tested in all five credential states, because a regulator will ask.

Public keys pass the gate too. The scaffolder's fork run found that Anvil's well-known accounts 0 and 1, whose private keys are printed by every local node start, pass `isAllowed` on both 151 and 153; somebody verified the public Hardhat keys (question 30, with Redbelly). Pre-flight warns on any of the ten default accounts whatever `isAllowed` says. I would deploy only from a keystore account.

Businesses are wallets too, but differently. A verified business gets a BusinessIdentifier contract whose delegate wallets have write access as sub-accounts, without their own KYC. Development and deployment wallets belong there rather than under a person. The [identity page](/identity/) covers it.
